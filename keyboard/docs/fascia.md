# Front fascia: the swappable palm rest

The keyboard is two parts. The **keyboard frame** (the back 122 mm) holds the keys, battery, controller and underside bays. The **front fascia** (the front 99 mm) is the palm rest and trackpad. It joins the frame along a straight seam, held by two release latches on the underside, with a small connector for the trackpad.

Why a separate part:

- Hands differ. One trackpad position can't suit everyone.
- The palm rest takes the most wear: sweat, oils, scratches. It should be cheap to replace without replacing the keyboard.
- Materials can change without touching the rest of the design.

## Materials

| Material | Why |
|---|---|
| **Aluminium** (default) | Matches the frame. Hard-anodised and sealed to resist sweat, but still metal under the wrists. |
| **Glass** | One sheet across the whole palm rest, trackpad included. Doesn't react with sweat, doesn't stain, wipes clean. |
| **Soft-touch polymer** | No metal contact at all, warmer to rest on, and the cheapest part to replace when it wears. |

## Built for big and sweaty hands

Accidental trackpad touches and sweat damage are two of the most common complaints about laptop-style keyboards, especially for people with large hands or very sweaty hands (hyperhidrosis). Deck One handles both:

**Accidental touches**
- Big-hands fascia: the trackpad sits 18.6 mm below the keys (vs 5.3 mm) behind a 1 mm thumb ridge you can feel.
- Typing-aware firmware: the trackpad ignores touches for a short moment after each keystroke (default 250 ms, adjustable).
- Palm rejection on large contact areas.
- **fn + esc** locks the trackpad completely, with a small light to show it's off.
- Or skip the trackpad entirely with the wrist-rest fascia.

**Sweat**
- Glass or soft-touch polymer palm rests: no bare metal under the wrists.
- PBT keycaps: they don't go shiny the way ABS caps do.
- A sealed membrane layer under the keys keeps moisture off the switches.
- Conformal coating on the circuit board.
- The fascia is a replaceable part, so wear doesn't mean a new keyboard.

## Making your own fascia

The seam is a straight line 122 mm from the back edge. Any part that is 304 mm wide and 99 mm deep, matches the wedge (7.4 mm thick at the seam, 4.5 mm at the front edge) and uses the two latch points can be a fascia: a numeric keypad, a larger trackpad, a wooden rest, a cooling gel pad. The top views in [`../drawings`](../drawings) show the seam line.
